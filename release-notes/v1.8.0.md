# Lanyard v1.8.0 — Mirror and control, and devices that find each other

## Mirror and control any screen

- Every phone and tablet in **Devices › Your devices** has **Mirror & control**. Tap it on
  the tablet to see the phone's screen full size and use it by touch: tap, hold, drag
  and scroll right on the picture, with Back, Home and Recents underneath. It works the
  other way round too, phone → tablet.
- The other device starts sharing by itself. Android shows its **Start now** prompt
  there (or a notification if the screen is off); tap it once. Android asks this every
  time screen sharing starts, and no app can skip it.
- On the PC: **Devices › Live › Mirror a phone or tablet…** does the same in a window.
  Click, drag and scroll on the picture to use the device.
- Pictures go straight between the two devices when they share a network, and through
  the PC otherwise.

- **Sound comes along.** On Android 10 and later, what apps play on the mirrored device
  (videos, music, games) plays on the device watching it, and in the PC window. There's a
  mute button. The mirrored device needs the microphone permission, and apps that block
  capture stay silent.
- Mirroring runs at up to 12 pictures a second, up from 8.

## No more scanning back and forth

- **Your devices find each other.** Every phone and tablet paired with your PC gets the
  same family key from it. On the same Wi-Fi they discover each other and link directly,
  with nothing to scan. Each keeps its PC connection at the same time.
- **Without a PC**, pair two devices once by PIN: **Devices › Nearby** lists Lanyard
  devices on the network. Tap **Pair** and type the PIN shown on the other device. It is
  remembered from then on.
- The QR code is still there under **Show a QR code instead**, for a tablet that should
  use a phone as its PC.
- Mirroring, the trackpad, files and clipboard all use whichever link is available:
  direct, or through the PC.

## Compatibility and Versioning

- Android app version: 1.8.0 (version code 33). Adds the full-screen-intent permission,
  used only for the "wants to see this screen" prompt.
- Windows companion and server protocol version: 1.8.0. The PC now hands its family key
  to paired devices.
- Upgrade every device to 1.8.0 so they all share one family. Pairing, settings and
  recordings carry over.
